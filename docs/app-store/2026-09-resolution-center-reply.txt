GUIDELINE 4.3(b) — DESIGN: SPAM

We understand the concern. A dating app that matches only on photos and
distance is hard to distinguish from many others already on the App Store,
and that was a fair description of the build you reviewed.

We have not answered this with cosmetic changes. Flame's matching premise is
now cross-language dating: members are matched on how their languages
complement one another, and a conversation between two people with no shared
language is translated automatically rather than requiring them to ask for it.

What is in build 10004:

1. Members declare the languages they speak and the languages they are
learning. This is part of creating an account, and existing members set it
from Edit Profile. The catalogue is 182 languages, shown with their own
endonyms (Oʻzbek, ئۇيغۇرچە, 한국어) rather than English names.

2. Those declarations are a weighted input to matching, not a filter or a
badge. Language complementarity carries 0.20 of the ranking score, alongside
shared interests, distance, recent activity and mutual preference
compatibility. Someone who speaks what you are learning, and is learning what
you speak, ranks above someone equally close and equally active who does not.

3. The languages are visible where the decision is made — on the card in the
deck, on the full profile, and on the member's own profile. Where the fit is
mutual, the card says so: a card whose owner speaks what you are learning and
is learning what you speak is marked "You can teach each other". That is the
one case the ranking scores highest, and it is marked only in that case.

4. Chat translates by default across a genuine language gap. When both people
have declared spoken languages and share none, incoming messages arrive
already translated, marked with a translation icon and a "Hide translation"
control. Where the two share a language nothing is translated and the manual
per-message option is unchanged. We default on only for a known mismatch: an
undeclared language is not evidence of a gap.

We have also renamed the app. "Flame Dating App: Meet & Date" stated the
category and nothing else; it is now "Flame: Date Across Languages", subtitle
"Chat translates automatically", in every localization.

To confirm this in about two minutes, signed in as the demo account:

  1. Open Discover. The cards show each person's languages. The demo
     account's matches include a Korean speaker learning English and a
     Spanish speaker learning English.
  2. Both cards carry a "You can teach each other" marker, because each of
     them speaks a language the demo account is learning and is learning the
     language it speaks. It is the ranking's reasoning, shown on the card.
  3. Tap a card to open the full profile and see the languages in full.
  4. Open a conversation with either of them. Because the demo account speaks
     English and neither of them does, their messages appear already
     translated, with a "Hide translation" control — no tap needed.
  5. Open Profile > Edit Profile > Languages to see and change the
     declaration that drives all of the above.


GUIDELINE 2.1 — UNABLE TO SIGN IN WITH THE DEMO ACCOUNT

Apologies for the wasted attempt. The credentials were correct; the sign-in
form was not. Pasting the address carried invisible characters with it — zero
width spaces and a byte-order mark — and the form compared the pasted string
literally, so it never matched. Typing it by hand worked, which is why this
passed our own testing.

The app now strips those characters from every field where an address is
typed or pasted: sign-in, registration, and both steps of password reset. The
same credentials in App Store Connect now work when pasted.


GUIDELINE 4 — SIGN IN WITH APPLE

You are right, and the cause was on our side rather than in the framework.

The app requested the fullName scope from Authentication Services, received
the name, and then discarded it — only the identity token and authorization
code were forwarded to our server. With no name to store, the account was
created as "New User", and the profile step presented that placeholder for the
user to correct. That is the behaviour you saw.

Build 10004 captures givenName and familyName from the Apple credential and
forwards them, so an account created through Sign in with Apple carries the
name Apple supplied and the user is not asked for it. The name only ever fills
a gap: on later sign-ins the existing account is returned untouched, so a name
the user has since edited is never overwritten.

One thing worth knowing before you test. Apple returns givenName and
familyName exactly once — on an Apple ID's first authorization of this app —
and never again. If you signed in to Flame with the same Apple ID during the
previous review, Apple will not send the name a second time and the app will
have nothing to display, so the original behaviour would appear unchanged.

To see the corrected behaviour, please use an Apple ID that has not signed in
to Flame before, or revoke the previous authorization first:

  Settings > [your name] > Sign-In & Security > Sign in with Apple > Flame >
  Stop Using Apple ID

The next sign-in is then treated as a first authorization and the name
arrives. We are glad to supply a dedicated Apple ID if that is easier.


GUIDELINE 2.1(a) — THE "SKIP FOR NOW" BUTTON

Reproduced and fixed.

The button was not dropping taps — it was disabled. The interests step
required at least one selection before allowing anyone to continue, and "Skip
for now" was wired to the same condition as "Continue", so the one control
that existed to bypass the requirement was itself blocked by it. It also still
rendered in its enabled colours, so nothing on screen explained why tapping
did nothing.

Both faults are fixed in build 10004. "Skip for now" is always actionable, and
the shared button component now takes its colours from whether it can actually
be pressed, so a disabled control can no longer look enabled. An automated
test covers the case and quotes your report, so it cannot regress.


GUIDELINE 2.1 — FACE DATA

Flame uses on-device face detection for one purpose: to check that a photo a
member uploads as a profile photo actually contains a human face.

What face data is collected: a camera or gallery image the member has chosen
is analysed in memory, on their own device, to detect whether a face is
present, plus coarse quality signals (eyes open, smiling) used only to reject
an unclear photo. No facial landmark data, faceprint, or biometric template is
created.

Planned use: solely to accept or reject that photo as a profile photo. There
is no other use and none planned.

Sharing: none. The analysis is performed by Google ML Kit running locally on
the device. No image or derived data is transmitted to Google, to us, or to
any third party for this check.

Third parties and storage location: none, and nowhere. Processing is transient
and in memory on the user's device. Nothing derived from face detection is
transmitted or stored on any server.

Retention: none. The pass/fail result is used at that moment and discarded.
The photo itself, once accepted, is stored as an ordinary profile photo and is
deleted through the normal photo and account deletion flows.

Deletion: not applicable — nothing derived from face detection is retained, so
there is nothing to delete beyond the profile photo itself.

Where this is explained in the privacy policy: section 5, "Photos and face
detection", at https://flamedating.net/privacy.

The specific text concerning face data reads:

  "We do not run face recognition, we do not compute a faceprint or any
  biometric identifier, we do not compare your face against any database,
  and photos are not sent anywhere for this purpose."
